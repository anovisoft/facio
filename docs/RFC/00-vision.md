# Vision

## The problem we actually solve

People do not fail because information is scarce. They fail because:

1. **Intention is vague** — “I want to get fit” is not a project.
2. **The first mile is cognitively expensive** — what to do tomorrow is unclear.
3. **Life interrupts** — illness, travel, boredom, shame, schedule collapse.
4. **Feedback is delayed or invisible** — especially for slow goals (weight, savings, skills).
5. **Tools fragment the job** — chat for ideas, notes for plans, calendar for reminders, habit apps for streaks — none owns *completion*.

Facio’s job is to own the gap between **want** and **done**.

## North star

> Any person can state an intention in natural language and receive a living micro-project that actively moves them toward a verifiable outcome — with AI as infrastructure, not the product surface.

## Product category (proposed)

Not “AI chat.” Not “productivity.” Not “habits.”

**Category claim:** *Outcome OS* / *Intention-to-Execution Platform*.

If we cannot defend a distinct category, we will be compared (and killed) as “ChatGPT + tasks.”

## Wedge (critical)

“Any intention” is a **terrible** launch wedge. It maximizes:

- blueprint coverage debt
- safety/liability surface
- quality variance
- marketing fuzziness

### Recommended wedge sequence

| Phase | Domain | Why |
|-------|--------|-----|
| 0 | **Skill acquisition with clear practice loops** (pull-ups, swimming basics, cooking one dish) | Easy evidence of progress; short cycles |
| 1 | **Fitness body-comp / training programs** | High willingness to pay; strong structure |
| 2 | **Habit cessation / behavior change** (smoking, doomscrolling) | Coach layer becomes valuable |
| 3 | **Finance micro-goals** (save X, pay debt Y) | Needs different evidence model |
| 4 | **Creative/long projects** (write a book) | Only after execution engine is mature |

**Decision rule:** expand domains only when previous domain’s completion rate and retention beat a predefined bar.

## Success definition

Facio wins when users attribute outcomes to the app:

- “I finished the carbonara path.”
- “I did my first unassisted pull-up.”
- “I stayed on plan after I got sick because Facio rebuilt week 3.”

Not when users say “cool chat” or “nice plan.”

### Primary metrics (vision-level)

| Metric | Definition | Why |
|--------|------------|-----|
| **Activation** | User reaches Day-1 action within 24h of intent | Plans without first action are waste |
| **Execution rate** | % of scheduled actions completed | Core product health |
| **Repair success** | % of stalled projects recovered within 7 days | Coach/value proof |
| **Outcome attainment** | % of projects reaching defined success criteria | North-star truth |
| **Gross margin / active project** | Cost to serve vs revenue | Survival |

Vanity metrics to deprioritize early: messages sent, tokens used, “plans generated.”

## Non-goals (vision boundaries)

Facio is **not** trying to be:

- a general assistant for email/calendar/life admin
- a social network of accountability buddies (maybe later, never core)
- a content publisher / course marketplace first
- a medical device or clinical treatment system
- an open-ended therapist chatbot

## Long-term vision (realistic version)

Over years, Facio accumulates:

- a curated **outcome taxonomy**
- versioned **blueprints** with adaptation rules
- anonymized **execution telemetry** (what works under which constraints)
- a **repair playbook library** (what to do when life breaks the plan)

The dream of “100,000 intents” is reframed as:

> A living library of **outcome types × constraint profiles × proven strategies**, continuously improved by real completion data.

That is a compounding asset. A dump of near-duplicate intent strings is not.

## Vision test

For any feature proposal, ask:

1. Does this increase probability of **outcome attainment**?
2. Does this reduce **time-to-first-action**?
3. Does this reduce **cost-to-serve** at scale?
4. Would this still matter if LLMs got 10× cheaper tomorrow?

If (4) is “no,” the feature is LLM-theater, not product.
